Definition

VR

Virtual reality. The headset track: rendering budgets, comfort, and what happens to a demo when the person wearing it has never held a controller before.

AR

Augmented reality. Overlays on the world you are already standing in, and the unglamorous work of making them readable in direct sunlight.

Virtuelle datacentre

The racks underneath all of it. Capacity planning, power draw, cooling, and the bill that arrives at the end of the quarter.

Fit

Left & Right

The venue is a converted machine hall ten minutes from the central station. It has good light, terrible acoustics in one corner, and a canteen that has been feeding people in this neighbourhood since long before any of us had an opinion about frame rates. We keep coming back to it because the hallway is wide enough that conversations do not block the door, which turns out to matter more than the size of the screen.

Talks are thirty minutes, hard stop, and we mean it: there is a clock facing the stage and a person next to it who is not afraid of you. Questions happen in the hallway afterwards, where they belong. If you want to demo something, bring it to the tables by the window rather than putting it on a slide, and expect somebody to pick it up and try to break it within the first minute.

Box

Tickets include both days, lunch, and coffee that is refilled without you having to ask. They do not include the after party, because there is no after party: everybody goes to the same bar across the street and that arrangement has worked fine since 2019.

About KladdleCon

KladdleCon is a two-day conference in Copenhagen for the people who actually build and run the things: the headsets, the models, and the racks underneath them. Two tracks, one hallway, and no talk longer than thirty minutes. We started in 2019 with sixty people in a borrowed canteen, and we are still trying to make it feel like that.
